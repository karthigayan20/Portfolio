Business problem
Signup volume was growing but paid conversion sat flat at 7.00%. Marketing was blamed for lead quality before anyone measured the product funnel.
Objective

14,200 user rows: eight funnel flags, signup cohort, channel, device, account segment, onboarding path, days to activation, MRR.

Why the leak costs so much
→

Accounts that activate convert at 20.3%

Accounts that never publish a first report convert at 1.5%. That is a 14× difference, and it means every user lost before activation is lost revenue, not just a lost step.

Against 76.3% for solo accounts. Only 26.9% of all signups ever invite anyone, so this is an under-used retention lever.

The costed fix

Conservative sizing

Opportunity

A 10-point improvement at one step is worth $300k in annualised revenue.

10,518 users reach the listing-connection step in six months and 5,208 of them fail it. Recovering 10 points of that — not the whole gap — carries 168 additional paying accounts through the rest of the funnel at the rates observed in the data.

Method & honesty notes

How the numbers were produced

Approach

  • User-level table generated with conditional step probabilities, so segment effects compound the way they do in a real product.
  • Funnel, step conversion, segment cuts and cohorts all computed from one SQL pattern so the totals reconcile.
  • Sizing built on observed downstream rates rather than a target, and deliberately set at 10 points rather than closing the whole gap.

Limits of this analysis

  • Simulated dataset, published so the full analysis can be inspected.
  • Segment differences here are correlational. The device finding needs a real experiment before anyone spends engineering time on it.
  • Six cohorts is enough for D90 on the earliest ones only; later cohorts are censored.
  • No pricing-page or in-app event data, so intent inside the step is invisible.
