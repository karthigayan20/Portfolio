Where should the next marketing dollar go?

One year of spend across six channels, reduced to the five numbers a marketing lead actually decides on — CAC, payback, ROAS, lead-to-customer rate and share of spend versus share of customers. The answer turned out to be a reallocation, not a bigger budget.

Business problem
Spend rose 18% across the year over six channels, and nobody could say which were carrying the growth and which were being subsidised.
Objective
Find the channels with the worst unit economics and reallocate budget without increasing total spend.
Dataset
72 rows: 6 channels × 12 months, with spend, impressions, clicks, leads, qualified leads, new customers and revenue. Published as CSV.
Tools
SQL for the metric layer, Excel for the model, Python for the scenario, hand-built dashboard for the read-out.

Recommendation, costed

Reallocation at flat spend

Move 60% of the LinkedIn Ads budget — $73k — into partner acquisition, lifecycle email and SEO. Incremental cost per customer in the receiving channels is set at 2.5–3.6× their current CAC, and each is capped by a growth ceiling, so the model does not assume cheap customers scale forever.

Why LinkedIn is reduced, not switched off

LinkedIn has the worst acquisition cost here, but in the funnel analysis its signups convert to paid at 8.31% — second best of any channel. It is buying fewer, better-qualified accounts. Cutting it entirely would remove that quality, so the recommendation keeps 40% of the budget as a targeting and creative test with a defined CAC exit threshold.

Key insights

Five findings
01

One channel carries a quarter of the budget and a twentieth of the customers

LinkedIn Ads costs $928 per customer — 4.0× the blended average — with a 8.6 months payback. It is 17.1% of spend and 4.3% of customers.

02

Partner acquisition is the most under-funded channel in the mix

Partner and agency referral produces 30.6% of all new customers on 11.6% of spend, at $87 CAC. It is the only cheap channel that scales with investment rather than list size.

03

Paid search and social absorb the majority of spend for a minority of customers

Google and Meta together take 60.4% of budget and return 37.7% of customers. Acceptable as volume channels, but they set the blended CAC floor and should be held flat, not grown.

04

Acquisition cost is seasonal, and the budget was not

Blended CAC peaks at $275 in November 2025 against $204 in July 2026 — a 35% swing on flat spend. Shifting budget out of the peak and into the trough is free efficiency.

05

The cheapest channel cannot absorb more money

Lifecycle email acquires at $48, but it is limited by list size, not budget. The right action is list growth and better triggers, not a bigger line item — which is why the model caps it at 62 additional customers.

Method & honesty notes

How the numbers were produced

Approach

  • Channel-month fact table generated with seasonality, channel-specific CTR/CPC profiles and realistic lead-to-customer rates.
  • Metric layer written in SQL: CTR, CPC, CPL, CAC, ROAS, lead-to-customer, payback.
  • Excel model reproduces the same metrics with live formulas so a stakeholder can change an assumption and watch it move.
  • Reallocation scenario modelled in Python with incremental CAC multiples and per-channel growth ceilings.

Limits of this analysis

  • The dataset is simulated so it can be published openly. The method, SQL and model are the same ones I use on client data.
  • Last-touch attribution: no multi-touch or incrementality testing, so paid channels likely take credit organic deserves.
  • Revenue is first-year and churn-adjusted, not lifetime value.
  • Non-paid "spend" is production and commission cost, so those CAC figures are not directly comparable with media CAC.
